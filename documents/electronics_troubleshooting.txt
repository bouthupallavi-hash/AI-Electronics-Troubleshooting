ELECTRONICS TROUBLESHOOTING KNOWLEDGE BASE

POWER SUPPLY PROBLEMS

Problem: Circuit does not turn on.

Possible causes:
- Power supply is disconnected.
- Battery is discharged.
- Power switch is OFF.
- Power wires are loose.
- Fuse may be open.
- There may be a short circuit.

Troubleshooting:
1. Check whether the power source is connected.
2. Check the power switch.
3. Check the supply voltage using an appropriate measuring instrument.
4. Inspect wires and connectors for loose connections.
5. Check the fuse if the circuit contains one.
6. If a short circuit or dangerous voltage is suspected, disconnect power and contact a qualified technician.


RESISTOR PROBLEMS

Problem: Resistor becomes very hot.

Possible causes:
- Excessive current is flowing.
- The resistor value may be incorrect.
- The resistor power rating may be too low.
- A nearby component may be faulty.

Troubleshooting:
1. Disconnect power before inspecting the circuit.
2. Check the resistor value using a multimeter.
3. Compare the resistor value with the circuit design.
4. Check whether the resistor power rating is suitable.
5. Check for possible short circuits or incorrect connections.


LED PROBLEMS

Problem: LED does not turn on.

Possible causes:
- LED is connected in the wrong polarity.
- Resistor is missing or incorrect.
- Power supply is unavailable.
- LED may be damaged.
- Wiring may be incorrect.

Troubleshooting:
1. Turn off the power.
2. Check the LED polarity.
3. Check the series resistor.
4. Check the supply voltage.
5. Inspect the wiring.
6. If necessary, test the LED using an appropriate circuit.


CAPACITOR PROBLEMS

Problem: Circuit behaves unexpectedly because of a capacitor.

Possible causes:
- Incorrect capacitor value.
- Incorrect polarity for a polarized capacitor.
- Capacitor may be damaged.
- Poor connection.

Troubleshooting:
1. Turn off the power.
2. Check the capacitor markings.
3. Verify the capacitance value.
4. For polarized capacitors, verify polarity.
5. Inspect the connections.
6. Replace a suspected faulty capacitor with a suitable component.


DIODE PROBLEMS

Problem: Diode circuit does not work correctly.

Possible causes:
- Diode polarity is reversed.
- Wrong diode type.
- Diode may be damaged.
- Connections may be incorrect.

Troubleshooting:
1. Disconnect power.
2. Identify the diode terminals.
3. Check the diode orientation.
4. Verify that the diode type is suitable.
5. Test the diode using a multimeter diode-test function if available.


TRANSISTOR PROBLEMS

Problem: Transistor circuit is not switching correctly.

Possible causes:
- Incorrect transistor pin connections.
- Incorrect biasing.
- Wrong transistor type.
- Damaged transistor.
- Power supply problem.

Troubleshooting:
1. Turn off the power.
2. Check the transistor part number.
3. Verify the pin configuration using the component datasheet.
4. Check the surrounding resistor connections.
5. Check the supply voltage.
6. If the transistor may be damaged, test or replace it with a suitable component.


OP-AMP PROBLEMS

Problem: Operational amplifier circuit gives an incorrect output.

Possible causes:
- Incorrect power supply.
- Incorrect input connections.
- Feedback connection is wrong.
- Component values are incorrect.
- Op-amp may be damaged.

Troubleshooting:
1. Check the op-amp power connections.
2. Check input connections.
3. Check the feedback network.
4. Verify resistor values.
5. Compare the circuit with its schematic.
6. Check the manufacturer's datasheet for the correct pin configuration.


MOTOR PROBLEMS

Problem: DC motor does not rotate.

Possible causes:
- No power supply.
- Loose wiring.
- Motor driver problem.
- Motor may be damaged.
- Insufficient supply current.

Troubleshooting:
1. Disconnect power before checking wiring.
2. Inspect motor connections.
3. Check the motor driver connections.
4. Verify that the supply is suitable for the motor.
5. Check whether the motor shaft can rotate normally.
6. If the motor requires high current or the circuit uses dangerous voltage, contact a qualified technician.


GENERAL TROUBLESHOOTING METHOD

When an electronics circuit does not work:

1. Understand what the circuit is supposed to do.
2. Check the power supply.
3. Inspect the wiring and connectors.
4. Check component orientation and polarity.
5. Check component values.
6. Measure important voltages using an appropriate instrument.
7. Compare measured values with expected values.
8. Test one section of the circuit at a time.
9. Replace a suspected faulty component only after checking the circuit.
10. For mains voltage, high voltage, large capacitors, or other dangerous circuits, disconnect power and contact a qualified technician.

SAFETY

Always disconnect power before modifying or inspecting a circuit when appropriate.

Do not work on exposed mains-voltage or high-voltage circuits without proper training and supervision.

Use appropriate test equipment and follow the manufacturer's safety instructions.

If a circuit involves dangerous voltage, large energy storage, burning components, smoke, or other hazardous conditions, stop troubleshooting and contact a qualified technician.
